# Florrie Production Audit — 15 June 2026

Read-only audit of the live Florrie SaaS (`~/ai-company/projects/florrie-ai`). No code was changed. Every claim below cites a `file:line`, a reproduction, or a build artefact. JARVIS was not touched.

Method: backbone files read directly (App.jsx, backend/index.js, design system, config), then six parallel deep-dives (money/reliability, security/privacy, route map, design/onboarding/iOS, and two page-by-page sweeps covering all 80+ pages). High-severity findings were spot-verified by hand against the source.

---

## 1. Executive summary and verdict

Florrie is further along than most pre-revenue SaaS. The hard, unglamorous parts are genuinely built and genuinely real: the public booking funnel, the inbox, Stripe Connect with deposits and off-session policy fees, WhatsApp connection, onboarding, the Setup Hub, exports, and the legal pages are all wired to real data with proper loading, empty, and error states. Auth and tenant isolation are solid. Performance and code-splitting are good. The copy is warm and on-brand.

But it is not ready to scale toward millions, and the gating issues are concrete.

The product has been built **wide, not deep**. There are 80+ pages and roughly half of the secondary ones are either mock-data fallbacks, real pages with fabricated metric strips bolted on, or real pages with dead buttons. A solo beautician who explores past the five core tabs will repeatedly hit screens that show invented numbers ("Recovered £2,740", "37% higher lifetime value", a fake staff leaderboard) or buttons that do nothing. That is the single biggest threat to paying-customer trust, and trust is the whole product here because Florrie acts on a client's behalf and charges their card.

Three things must be fixed before any growth push:

1. **A live double-charge bug.** The manual no-show charge route has no idempotency and shares no guard with the automatic policy-fee path, so a client's real card can be charged twice (`stripe.js:407`). This is a refund-and-churn event waiting to happen. Blocker.
2. **Mock data shown to real users.** Several pages render fabricated revenue, staff, and engagement numbers, indistinguishable from real data at a glance. This is exactly the failure mode the brief warned about, and it is back at scale. Blocker for credibility.
3. **The "wide and shallow" surface.** 20 pages are unreachable from the UI, two nav buttons 404, and dozens of buttons across otherwise-real pages have no handler. The app feels half-finished the moment you leave the happy path.

Verdict: **strong foundation, not shippable to scale as-is.** The core loop (book, confirm, remind, complete, get paid) works. The surrounding 70% of the app needs a ruthless cut-or-fix pass before the product can survive a paying user poking around. None of this is a rewrite. It is a focused remediation sprint on a fundamentally sound base.

## 3. Route, navigation, and endpoint map (Step 1)

### How a user actually navigates
The bottom nav exposes only five destinations: Today, Inbox, the centre petal (Talk to Florrie, `/voice`), Content, and Money (`App.jsx:494-521`). Everything else is reached through the floating "More" pill, which opens `/more`. `More.jsx` lists a curated catalogue, and on 2026-06-10 a block of pages was deliberately "parked" out of that menu (`More.jsx:119-127`). Parking removed their only entry point, so they are now dead UI.

### Orphan pages: 20 fully unreachable
No nav entry, no menu entry, nothing in the app navigates to them. Reachable only by typing the URL.

`/dashboard`, `/escalations`, `/approval-queue`, `/feedback`, `/notes`, `/checklist`, `/inventory`, `/referrals`, `/portfolio`, `/packages`, `/tags`, `/goals`, `/client-timeline`, `/api-settings`, `/messaging`, `/ai-insights`, `/client-intel`, `/integrations`, `/demand`, plus `/segments` and `/churn` (reachable only via the already-unreachable `/client-intel`, so transitively orphaned).

Several of these are real, working pages (Escalations, ApprovalQueue) that a beautician simply cannot find. ApprovalQueue is the human-in-the-loop control for an AI that sends messages on her behalf, and it has no way in.

### Dead links: 2 (both reach NotFound)
- `Dashboard.jsx:491` — `navigate('/florrie')`, no such route. The "View full activity log" button 404s.
- `Clients.jsx:559` — `navigate('/clients/import')`, no such route (it is `/import`). The empty-state import action 404s. This one is on a reachable page.

### Duplicate / overlapping destinations: 5 groups
- **Dashboard vs Hub.** Two full home screens. Hub is live (wired to nav, warmed, holds the activity feed). Dashboard is the orphaned pre-2026-05-28 Stitch screen and carries the broken `/florrie` link. Retire Dashboard.
- **Calendar surface.** Six routes (`/`, `/hub`, `/today`, `/calendar`, `/calendar/week`, `/smart-schedule`) all render `<Hub/>`. The direct `CalendarView` import and warm in `App.jsx:17,188` are redundant; CalendarView is still live, but only because `Hub.jsx:131` embeds it for the week view.
- **Messaging triplet.** `/inbox` (live) vs `/messaging` (orphan overview) vs `/comms` (redirects to inbox). Plus two template editors: `/templates` (parked) and `/whatsapp/templates` (in menu).
- **Client-intelligence quintet.** `/client-intel`, `/segments`, `/churn`, `/ai-insights`, `/demand` all overlap and only cross-link each other. Consolidation candidate.
- **Analytics aliases.** `/reports`, `/digest`, `/treatment-stats` all redirect to `/analytics`. This one is clean and is the pattern to copy.

### Endpoint map
Most data flows go through Supabase directly from the browser (RLS-gated); the backend handles AI actions, payments, webhooks, exports, and messaging. Cross-referencing frontend calls against the 42 backend mounts:
- **No live frontend call hits a missing backend endpoint.** The one apparent miss (`/api/patch-tests/stats` in `Compliance.jsx:69`) is a code comment, with a real Supabase fallback.
- **Dead backend endpoint group:** `/api/hmrc/*` (5 auth-protected endpoints) has zero frontend callers. HMRC MTD shipped server-only with no UI.
- **Auth posture is clean.** Every CRUD route applies `requireAuth`. The only unauthenticated routes are webhooks (HMAC or token verified), public booking/course enrollment, and the Turnstile-gated waitlist POST.

### Structural finding
`frontend/src/pages/backend/` is a misplaced 44-file copy of the entire backend (its own `package.json`, `Dockerfile`, `.env.example`, all routes, migrations, services). Nothing imports it and it is gitignored, but it sits inside the React page tree, inflates greps, and is a latent footgun if a bundler glob ever widens. Delete it.

## 5. Cross-cutting findings

### 5.1 Design and consistency (4/10)
A real design system exists (`designSystem.js`, `theme.jsx`) with good tokens and light/dark CSS variables, but it is decorative, not enforced.
- Only **11 of 84 pages import the design system**. There are **4,358 hardcoded hex values** across `pages/`. The gradient rose `#C76B8A` is pasted by hand 502 times; a text colour `#2D2A26` appears 381 times and does not even match the theme's `--text-primary: #1d1b19`. 61 pages define their own private `const S` style object.
- Consequence: a single accent or radius change cannot be made in one place, and light/dark mode is partially broken on any page that hardcodes hex instead of `var(--...)`.
- **Font stack contradicts itself.** `index.html` loads Fraunces first in `--font-display`, while `designSystem.js:18` says Playfair only. Six font families plus Material Symbols are loaded. The fallback brand colour even disagrees page-to-page (`#C76B8A` in Onboarding vs `#92405e` in SetupHub).
- **Em dashes: 97 in rendered UI copy** (351 occurrences across 82 files), a direct violation of the hard brand rule, including client-facing text at `ClientManagePage.jsx:246,415` and `Compliance.jsx:121`. The 2026-05-16 sweep only ever touched 5 files; the debt is fully back.
- The good news: copy tone is genuinely warm and on-brand, with only one slop word ("seamlessly") in the entire app.

### 5.2 Core journeys, end to end
- **Book to paid (public):** works. Multi-treatment, add-ons, deposits, Stripe redirect with hostname validation, three success states. One gap: a required `signature` consultation field cannot be answered (`BookingPage.jsx:1123`).
- **Book to confirmation to 24h reminder to complete to rebook:** mostly works, but the rebook nudge at the end of completion is a dead button (`CalendarView.jsx:1013`), which kills the retention moment the product is sold on. Reminders also fire on `pending` (unpaid) bookings that the 15-minute cleanup may then cancel (`notifications.js:1123`).
- **Inbound message to inbox to reply:** works well. Real threads, AI suggestions, channel auto-detect, optimistic send.
- **Deposit to policy fee to payout:** the automatic path is correct and idempotent. The manual no-show route is the Blocker (see 5.4).

### 5.3 Onboarding and activation (7/10)
A tight 6-step flow reaches first value (a live public booking link) in under three minutes, and the Setup Hub is honest: `setup.js` reads real tables with exact counts and fails soft. Two issues hold it back:
- The final button passes a click Event instead of a route (`Onboarding.jsx:822`).
- The hardest step, WhatsApp connect (around 15 minutes, needs a spare number), sits last. Many users will skip it and never return, and the SMS fallback has a history of blocked delivery. Activation is the biggest revenue lever, so the order is backwards.
- Minor: the Setup Hub marks client import "done" only at 5+ clients (`setup.js:126`), so importing 1 to 4 reads as incomplete.

### 5.4 The money loop
Verified correct: Stripe signature verification on both webhooks over raw body; the main webhook never returns 500 after verification; `chargePolicyFee` has a double guard plus a Stripe idempotency key; metering only counts confirmed sends; the Connect transfer/application-fee model and refund reversal; consistent integer-pence math.

The problems:
- **Blocker — no-show double-charge.** `POST /api/stripe/charge-no-show` (`stripe.js:345-470`) never checks `policy_fee_charged_at` or `no_show_fee_charged`, and its `paymentIntents.create` at `:407` has **no idempotency key**. The automatic policy-fee path already charges on the `no_show` status flip. The two paths share no guard, so marking no-show then tapping the manual charge (or a double-tap / retry of the manual route) charges the client's card twice for real.
- **High — non-atomic metering.** All three meter writers do read-modify-write in JS with no atomic increment or lock (`whatsapp-metering.js:123-207`, `sms-metering.js:91-114`). The nudge engines fan out over hundreds of clients, so concurrent sends lose increments. Because billing reads `overage_total_pence` off these rows, the drift is drift in what the card is charged.
- **High — two live Stripe webhooks.** `/api/billing/webhook` (`billing.js:208`) has no idempotency table and re-runs the switch on every Stripe retry, and returns 503 on a missing-signature case (`:216`) which can get the endpoint flagged. The code comments already admit both URLs may be configured. Decommission one.
- **High — `billMonthlySurplus` runs on every boot and on an interval** with no inter-instance lock (`index.js:445-452`). Safe today only because it bills frozen past-month rows; a stuck `billed=false` row retried after the 24h Stripe idempotency window expires would double-invoice.
- **Medium — legacy weekly SMS biller** (`sms-metering.js:251`) still exists with no idempotency key; if it is ever cron'd alongside the monthly biller it double-bills, because both meters are written on every send.
- **Medium — no-show cap is 2x price** (`stripe.js:376`), a chargeback magnet; `payment_intent.payment_failed` reconciles `no_show_fee` but not `late_cancel_fee` (`:887`).

### 5.5 Reliability
- 24h reminder guard is check-then-insert with no unique constraint (`notifications.js:1033`); rare under a single replica, but not a hard guard. Add `UNIQUE(appointment_id, action_type)`.
- ApprovalQueue marks actions executed before confirming the execute call succeeded (`ApprovalQueue.jsx:75`): a failed AI action looks successful.
- Maintenance jobs `bill-surplus-sms.js` and `cleanup-stripe-events.js` are not wired to any scheduler; cleanup also runs in-process, so do not assume the standalone jobs run.
- WaitlistPro throws a ReferenceError on load failure (`:108`).

### 5.6 Security and privacy (6/10)
The access-control story is genuinely strong. Every authenticated data route carries `requireAuth`; no cross-tenant IDOR was reachable in any `:id` mutation; the client bundle is clean (anon key plus publishable Stripe key only, `.env` gitignored); RLS migrations (005, 054) are correct. The real risks:
- **High — webhooks fail open when their secret is unset.** WhatsApp, Instagram, and Twilio-SMS verification is skipped, not rejected, if the app secret is missing (`webhooks.js:129`, `instagram-webhooks.js:60`, `webhooks.js:314`), and those secrets are in the warn-only `OPTIONAL_ENV` list, so the backend boots without them. A misconfigured deploy becomes an open ingestion endpoint that drives the AI and triggers outbound sends. Make verification fail closed in production.
- **High — a consultation-form bearer token is logged in plaintext** (`consultation-forms.js:474`). That token is the only credential guarding a client's health consultation form (special-category data under UK GDPR). Stop logging it.
- **Medium — Bird SMS webhook authed only by a query-string token** (`webhooks.js:499`): leaks into logs, plain `!==`, skipped if env unset.
- **Medium — service-role-everywhere architecture.** Every route uses the service-role key and bypasses RLS, so tenant isolation is 100% app-code. All handlers are correct today, but one forgotten `.eq('beautician_id')` is a full breach with no DB backstop. Add a lint/test guard.
- **Medium — PII in logs.** Pino has no redaction; phone numbers and inbound senders are written to prod logs in several files. Add a redact list.
- **Medium — PECR marketing guard fails open** on phone-match failure and never checks positive consent (`marketing-guard.js:61`).
- Low: delete the stray `frontend/src/pages/backend/` copy; Turnstile is globally disabled until wired; `/api/appointments/slots` is unauthenticated availability scraping.

### 5.7 Performance (8/10)
React.lazy splitting works (around 90 chunks); core bundle 663 KB raw / 201 KB gzip; the 377 KB SheetJS dependency is correctly isolated to ClientImport; route chunks are warmed after login; no N+1 query patterns in Clients, Analytics, or MoneyTracker. Total `dist` is 3.3 MB. The main avoidable cost is six web-font families in `index.html`.

### 5.8 iOS / Capacitor (6/10)
Permission strings are excellent (camera, mic, photos, speech), `aps-environment: production` is set, and native push is correctly wired alongside web push. Problems: the status bar requests light text on a near-white background and the styling function is never called, so it is both wrong and dead code (`native.js:21`, `capacitor.config.ts:29`); two different hexes are used for the same surface; there are no deep links or universal links; and safe-area insets are used in only three files, so hardcoded-padding pages will not respect the notch consistently.

### 5.9 Cross-app honesty bugs (fix before any user sees them)
- **Overage price stated four ways:** Pricing FAQ "7p", `subscription.js` "6p SMS / 5p WhatsApp", WhatsAppConfig "5p/6p", Terms "10p".
- **Team pricing** says £50/mo + £25/seat vs the real £29 + £15.
- **SMS allowance** copy says "30/week" vs the real 120/month.
- **Two privacy policies** with mismatched entity names ("Florrie Ltd" vs "FLORRIE.AI LTD").

## 6. The "millions a year" lens

**What makes a beautician pay and keep paying?** One thing: Florrie visibly saves her time and brings money in while she is with a client. The proof moments are the autonomous reminder that stops a no-show, the inbox reply she did not have to write, the rebook nudge that fills next month, and the policy fee that actually lands in her account. Florrie has built all four. The problem is they are buried or broken at the exact point of proof: the rebook nudge is a dead button, the activity feed that shows "what Florrie did" competes with mock screens that show what Florrie did not do, and ApprovalQueue (her control over the AI) is unreachable.

**Activation, retention, expansion.**
- Activation is good but front-loads the hardest step (WhatsApp) last. Move a "first win" earlier: import clients and fire one real reminder inside onboarding so she sees value before she is asked to wire up messaging.
- Retention hinges on the rebook loop and the daily inbox. Both are real, but the rebook send is dead and the inbox is one of only two reasons to open the app daily. This is where to invest.
- Expansion (seats, higher tiers, usage overage) is barely wired: Team pricing is wrong, the overage price is stated four ways, and there is no in-app moment that nudges an upgrade off real usage.

**Trust-breakers that cause churn or refunds.**
1. The double-charge bug. A single duplicate card charge on a client is a refund, an angry beautician, and likely a cancellation.
2. Mock data shown as real. The first time she spots a fabricated "£2,740 recovered" she stops believing every number in the app.
3. Dead buttons on the happy path (rebook, client message, copy payment link). Each one quietly says "this is not finished."

**Five changes, ranked by impact to effort, to move revenue.**
1. **Kill the double-charge (Blocker).** Add an idempotency key and a shared charged-guard to `charge-no-show`, ideally routing it through `chargePolicyFee`. Small change, removes the biggest churn-and-refund risk. Highest impact, lowest effort.
2. **De-mock the app.** Replace every mock-fallback and fabricated metric with a real value or an honest empty state (RevenueGoals, StaffPerformance, AutomationRules, ClientMemberships, CommsLog, ChurnPrevention, plus the hardcoded stat strips). Medium effort, restores the trust the whole product runs on.
3. **Wire the dead buttons on the core loop.** Rebook reminder, client Message/Send-Offer, payment-link copy, WaitlistPro Notify/Offer. Low effort each, directly serves the retention story.
4. **Cut the surface.** Delete or hide the 20 orphan pages and the duplicate Dashboard/CalendarView/messaging/client-intel stacks; fix the two dead links. A smaller, fully-working app beats a sprawling half-built one. Low to medium effort, big perceived-quality gain.
5. **Re-order onboarding for a first win and fix the final-button bug.** Deliver one real, visible Florrie action before asking for WhatsApp. Medium effort, lifts activation, which is the top revenue lever.

### High
