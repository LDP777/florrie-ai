Who she is

She's the colleague you always needed

Florrie reads your WhatsApp, Instagram DMs, texts, and emails. She replies in your voice. Not a chatbot voice. Yours. She writes "hiya lovely" because that's what you write. She knows your cancellation policy word for word. She knows you don't take bookings before 10 on Mondays.

She gets better every time you correct her. Changed a reply she sent? She remembers why and adjusts next time. After a week, your regulars won't notice the difference. New clients will think you reply fast.

Got an existing client list? Import it from a spreadsheet in seconds. Every name, number, and note carries over, Florrie knows who everyone is from day one, no manual entry required.

She books appointments, sends confirmations, chases no-shows, fills last-minute gaps from your waitlist, and nudges clients to rebook when they're due. All while you're with a client, or at the gym, or asleep.

How it works

Your morning. Reimagined.

7:30am
You're still in bed. Florrie's been up since six. Three WhatsApp messages came in overnight: a new booking request, a reschedule, and someone asking about lash lift prices. All handled. Two confirmations sent. One price list shared with a link to book.
9:15am
Your first client sits down. Your phone buzzes. Cancellation for 2pm. Florrie's already texted the next three people on your waitlist. By the time you look up from the brow, the slot's filled. You didn't touch your phone once.
12:00pm
Lunch. You snap a photo of a receipt from Sally Beauty. £34 for wax supplies. Florrie scans it, files it under HMRC consumables, and your running tax estimate ticks up. No spreadsheet. No shoebox. Thirty seconds and it's done.
2:30pm
A new enquiry lands on Instagram. "Do you do brow lamination? How much?" Florrie sends them straight to your WhatsApp with a friendly auto-reply. They message you there, Florrie handles it, and the client books for Thursday. You never saw either message.
4:00pm
Florrie flags something: Emma Collins has a tint booked for Friday, but her last patch test was eight weeks ago. Needs a fresh one. Florrie's already sent Emma a reminder with instructions and a link to confirm.
6:30pm
Your last client leaves. Florrie's sent three aftercare messages, nudged two people to rebook, and drafted five Instagram posts for this week, based on the treatments you did today, ready for your five-minute review. You approve two, tweak one, skip two. Florrie schedules them for peak hours and queues the rest. You close the salon door. The week's content is sorted.
